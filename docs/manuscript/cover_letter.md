# Cover Letter

Dear Editor,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript **"A transcriptome-wide cis-MR and colocalization atlas for type 2 diabetes, coronary artery disease, and fasting glucose: operating characteristics and candidate effector genes"** for consideration as an Article in the *American Journal of Human Genetics*.

The manuscript addresses a fundamental gap in post-GWAS gene prioritization: although transcriptome-wide Mendelian randomization (cis-MR) followed by Bayesian colocalization is now a standard tool for nominating effector genes, its operating characteristics—the rate at which MR-supported hypotheses reach strong colocalization, and how that rate calibrates with MR evidence—have not been systematically quantified. We performed a cis-MR × coloc screen of 31,371 gene–trait pairs across type 2 diabetes, coronary artery disease, and fasting glucose, and report three findings we believe are of broad interest to the human genetics community:

1. **Operating characteristics.** Strong-colocalization yield rises monotonically from 0.71% to 25.6% across the nominal MR p-value funnel, and applying the pre-registered per-outcome BH-FDR threshold (q<0.05) yields 121 strong colocalizations among 982 MR-supported pairs (12.3%; 95% CI 10.4–14.5%), converging with the stage-2 LD-clumping yield of 12.96%. This provides investigators with a principled calibration for interpreting coloc output.
2. **A decisive negative boundary.** Only 2/27,123 pairs with nominally non-significant MR (one-sided 95% upper bound 0.0232%/pair) support strong colocalization, constraining false-positive rates for a well-specified coloc prior.
3. **Reproduced and candidate effector genes.** All 106 previously reported strong colocalizations were reproduced, and 15 additional candidates were identified under the FDR core (9 candidate effector genes within 100–250 kb of known T2D/CAD loci, 6 without T2D/CAD catalog records), with directional replication in GTEx v8 (6/7 measurable), FinnGen R11 (8/9 variant-level, 9/9 gene-level, 4 with p<0.05), and overall sensitivity support from SMR+HEIDI (71.7% of known regions) and Steiger (96.1% of HEIDI-passing loci).

We have been explicit about limitations that we believe reviewers will value: only 38.7% of known colocalized regions reach genome-wide significance, a permutation-based false-positive estimate (1.45%) is reported as an upper-bound indicator, a coloc.susie sensitivity analysis with an external LD reference did not converge under default settings and was therefore reported as exploratory (one candidate, LAMC1, was excluded on multi-signal evidence and is disclosed transparently), and partial sample overlap between the eQTLGen cohort and the GWAS samples may inflate MR recall and colocalization coverage. All analyses used publicly available summary statistics; the colocalization atlas is provided as a public resource.

The manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration by another journal. The author has approved the manuscript and declares no competing interests.

Thank you for considering this work. I look forward to your response.
